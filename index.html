LAURA MAI STOINSKI. Hi, I am a Doctoral researcher at the Max Planck Research Group "Vision and Computational Cognition." My co-supervisors are Prof. Martin Hebart and Prof. Gesa Hartwigsen.

I am fascinated by how we perceive and represent the world around us. More specifically, my research examines the nature of internal object representations, such as our neural or mental representations of the concepts "dog," "peace," or "scientist."

Currently, my work is guided by three key questions: What are the core visual and semantic properties that enable us to recognize and differentiate objects? How are these properties mapped onto the human cortex? and What are the distinct contributions of visual input and semantic knowledge to visual object processing?

To address these questions, I compare the representations of object images with their corresponding nouns, and investigating how object representations differ between sighted and congenitally blind populations. My work integrates methods from neuroscience, computer science, and cognitive psychology to provide a comprehensive understanding of mind, brain and behavior.

THINGS is a freely available, large-scale database of 1,854 systematically sampled object concepts and 26,107 high-quality naturalistic images of these concepts. We recently extended THINGS by adding concept- and image-specific norms, 53 higher-level category memberships, and one license-free image per concept.

With this project, we hope to provide researchers with a valuable resource of normed living and non-living object stimuli. Many laboratories around the world have started collecting data with THINGS and made their data publicly available for research purposes. Interested in using THINGS? Check it out here ➔
